Read this first: the file generated this session must NOT be uploaded

gems62e-h62o-orgstack-oof-lidarprof-40000dots-20261012-2f20698c5cbd-zeros.tif is format-legal and isolated, but it fails the repository's own uniqueness gate (87.84 % of its dots within 3 px of GEMSDOE22__gems22-h23-b-dti-optimal-emission-1…, limit 70 %). Uploading it would (a) breach the parallel-run protocol this repository operates under, and (b) spend a weekly submission slot on a field with no evidence of a gain. The steps below are written so that a future session can follow them once a field actually passes.

1. The exact click path

  1. Sign in at https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/.
  2. Open Submit (also reachable from the submissions page) and upload the single .tif — or a .zip containing exactly one GeoTIFF.
  3. Paste the submission name and the short note (both below), then confirm.
  4. Wait for the portal's receipt. The receipt score is the only ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED number that exists. Nothing produced in this repository is organizer-confirmed, and no projection may ever be written into a score column.
  5. Record the receipt: the score, the UTC timestamp, the exact filename and its sha256, into research/leaderboard_series.json and the session's run card.

3. The unique submission filename

gems62e-h62o-orgstack-oof-lidarprof-40000dots-20261012-2f20698c5cbd-zeros.tif

Convention: <repo><lane>-h<hypothesis id>-<what it is>-<n dots>dots-<UTC date>-<first 12 hex of the file's own sha256>-<outside encoding>. The embedded hash makes the name self-authenticating: anyone can check that the bytes they downloaded are the bytes that were named, and two artifacts can never share a name. A NaN-outside twin is also published (gems62e-h62o-orgstack-oof-lidarprof-40000dots-20261012-2f20698c5cbd-nan.tif) for viewers that need the footprint encoded as NaN; upload the -zeros file, because NaN outside the footprint is one of the two mechanisms behind the portal's range rejection.

4. The regression this repository must never repeat

A previous upload was rejected with "Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]". Two mechanisms produce that message, and src/gems62/grid.py::write_submission removes both, then re-reads the bytes from disk and re-counts before returning a receipt:

  • a float32 sentinel such as -3.4028234663852886e+38 (the organiser stack's nodata) left anywhere in the array;
  • a NaN nodata tag written into the TIFF metadata, which the portal reads as an out-of-range value even when every finite pixel is inside [0, 1].

The shipped file carries no nodata tag at all and encodes "outside the footprint" as 0.0.

62e: 40k isolated dots, blocked OOF detector on the 19-band stack x LiDAR scarp. Uniqueness gate: DUPLICATE-STOP. Not for submission.

6. What a future session must do before spending a slot

  1. Beat the best local evidence. The protocol forbids spending a slot on an idea that has not beaten the current holdout best — and this session's own instruments are documented as unable to rank a new field at all (see why no gain can be certified). Any claim of improvement must be MEASURED, not MODEL.
  2. Clear the uniqueness gate mechanically: ρ ≤ 0.90 and ≤ 70 % within 3 px against every non-vacuous registry raster, checked on the surface before placement and on the final dots, plus the isolated-random control (without it the 3 px leg is density-blind — IR-62-22).
  3. Close the arrangement gap. Inside the M ≈ 37,000-44,000 band, every field scoring 0.26-0.2778 carries dens21 8.75-9.62 and coh21 0.307-0.336; this field carries 4.56 and 0.1593. Dots must lie along coherent corridors, and a hard-core selection of a smooth probability surface does not produce that (experiment 11 refuted the smooth fix).
  4. Keep the isolation: at least 400 m between any two dots, so no dot competes with another inside one 300 m kernel (self-kernel must stay ≈ 0).
  5. Keep it off the catalogue: catalogue fault pixels are masked out of scoring, so any dot on them is pure FP mass.
