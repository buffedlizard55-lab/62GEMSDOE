H62-K — continue the trace, do not buffer it

  • Layers. data/labels.tif (mapped trace geometry: orientation, endpoints, segment lengths), evidence/dem/lahontan_dem_utm11.tif (25 m), evidence/h62j/fields_100m.npz (the isobase surface and bench field already computed), plus qfault_attributes.csv / trace_segments_utm11.csv / gdr_qfaults_traces.csv for throw-class and segment length.
  • Signature. Beyond each mapped tip, along the extrapolated strike, a corridor in which the DEM response (bench cut, doublet step, or scarp curvature) exceeds the same statistic measured at equal distance from the trace's middle — i.e. a continuation test, not a proximity test.
  • Why it should catch a missing fault. Catalogue tips are where mapping stopped (coverage, cloud, vegetation, alluvium), not where rupture stopped; offset of an isobase across a continuation is the classic sub-scarp signature and requires no new geomorphology.
  • How it differs from every registry field. All 79 registry rasters are driven by perpendicular proximity or ridge/hillshade response. None is built on along-strike continuation of the catalogue's own geometry — which makes it structurally unique (the duplicate gate cannot fire on it) and keeps it inside the datum lane.
  • Kill test. Instrument-B credit of the continuation dots against mass-matched control dots drawn from the same catalogue-distance bands. If the ratio is under ~1.2× at whatever mass survives isolation, the lane's geometry is exhausted and this is written up as a second measured negative.

H62-L — the collar ladder (a measurement about the metric, not about faults)

Every lane here excludes a 250–300 m collar around the catalogue — including, note, one that a banker's-rounded int(round(250/100)) silently turned into 223.6 m (IR-62-20). The band table says credit is highest exactly where the collars cut. So: hold the detector fixed, emit at collars {200, 300, 500, 1000} m, and compare model DTI at fixed N. Cost is one sweep per lane, and it settles a group-wide policy question that has been argued from intuition for weeks. The honest complication is stated in advance: dots within ~200 m of a mapped trace are masked out of the organiser's truth, so any gain must come from the flanking damage zone; whether that zone reaches 300 m or 900 m is precisely what this experiment measures.

H62-M — measure the platform, not the scarp

A Lake Lahontan bench is a width as well as a height: its dip-direction extent scales with stillstand duration and with the slope it was cut into. A fault that interrupts the stillstand truncates the width while leaving bench elevations comparable on the two sides — the opposite of the doublet criterion that this session ran, which looks for a vertical offset. Layers: the 25 m DEM and the fitted isobase surface; signature: a step in bench width (measured perpendicular to the isobase, over a 4–8 km tile) co-located with a bench score that is high on both sides. Cheap: the fields are already on disk. Kill test as for H62-K.

H62-N — the residual is the deformation

This session fitted a robust modal bench elevation per 4 km tile (1,093 tiles with a bench, surface 1,159.05–1,453.25 m, evidence/h62j/isobase.json) so that the doublet measurement could subtract large-scale warp. The fit residual itself — measured surface minus a published Lahontan rebound or tilt model, not a locally smoothed version of the same data — is a deformation field, and its gradients may localise strain better than a two-flank step test does. Cost is hours and no new fetch; the risk is circularity, which is why the falsifier is fixed in advance: the residual-gradient field must beat the doublet field's 1.60× on the same domain and mass, or the lane's conclusion stands as written.

Two things this card does not propose

Not thermal occurrences as structure. The other lane's H62-Q specification (5 / 10 / 20 km buffers around GDR wells and springs, mass-matched control, "below 1.0× it dies") was run to the letter and it dies: 0.79× for Hot+Warm at every radius, 0.87–0.88× for Hot-only, 0.83× / 0.87× for vents at 5 and 10 km, and 1.13× at 20 km — which a placebo of five random 21-point buffer sets reproduces (0.04757 ± 0.00499 against the real 0.04966, z = +0.42). Occurrence cells themselves score 0.75–0.84×, 600 m corridors 0.77×, mapped vents 0.10–0.21×, and everything outside the buffers 0.60–0.64×. Evidence evidence/h62j2-geothermal.json. The only defensible thermal use left is as a weight on a structural candidate, and the burden of proof for that now sits with whoever proposes it.

Not more mass in the far field. The arithmetic is closed: DTI → 5 × mean per-dot credit, and this session's far-field credit is 0.018. Widening gates to reach 20,000–40,000 dots lowers mean credit further (run 1 → run 2: 8,571 dots at 1.74× became 10,870 at 1.60×); it moves the number, not the score.
