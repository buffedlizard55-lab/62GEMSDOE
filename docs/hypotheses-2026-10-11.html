Candidate hypotheses, ranked — 2026-10-11 session

The brief asks for 3–5 candidates not yet tried, each naming its layers, its physical signature, why it should catch a fault missing from the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue, and how it differs from anything already implemented. Ranked by expected DTI improvement against implementation cost.

Two of the five were screened in this session and are reported with their measured result, not with a hope. Screening instrument: SGMC 1:750 k off-catalogue truth (79,615 px, catalogue masked), emission of 44,090 dots under a 300 m hard-core, compared against an isolated-random control that scores 0.04281 on the same instrument. evidence/h62g/exp2.json, MEASURED.

H62-K — Along-strike continuation into catalogue gaps · rank 1

  • Layers: existing_faults.tif (mapped trace geometry only — orientation and endpoints, not fault presence), the 100 m DEM (geodawn_dem_a2_100m.tif, 99.97 % footprint coverage), and topo_10m_derived_100m_u8.tif bands hs_lineament (8) and curv_prof_absmax (6).
  • Signature: for every mapped segment, extrapolate its strike beyond each endpoint and score the corridor by DEM lineament response and along-corridor profile-curvature continuity; emit dots where the corridor has DEM support and the catalogue does not.
  • Why it catches a missing fault: the organizers state that a "new fault" includes newly mapped geometry of existing systems (thread 11536, quoted in research/sources.md). This is the only hypothesis that targets that population by construction: it is defined on catalogue gaps, so it cannot re-find a mapped fault, and it inherits the strike information of a population that is known to be real.
  • How it differs from this repo: every lane here treats the catalogue as a mask to be excluded. None uses its geometry as a prior. emit.py computes a catalogue distance transform, but only to push dots away, never to orient them.
  • Risk: the corridor prior can be wrong where a mapped segment terminates because the fault genuinely ends (a transfer zone or a bend), and the DEM's 100 m posting smooths the very low-amplitude scarps that matter.

H62-J — Differential-rebound gradient · rank 2, cheapest

  • Layers: the fitted isobase itself (src/gems62/datum.py), plus the 100 m DEM.
  • Signature: the second spatial derivative (Laplacian and maximum directional curvature) of the fitted rebound surface. Adams, Wesnousky & Bills (1999) attribute ~22 m of post-highstand deflection mostly to isostatic rebound with a small down-to-the-north tilt; the gradient of that field is largest where crustal load changed most, and strain localises at load gradients.
  • Why it catches a missing fault: it is a stress proxy rather than a geomorphic one, so it fires where displacement has not yet produced a mappable scarp — precisely the population the catalogue lacks.
  • How it differs: datum.py fits the isobase and only ever uses its residual to find steps. Its gradient field is computed nowhere.
  • Caveat that must be stated before any result is trusted: IR-62-03 records that the isobase fit is degenerate in its linear part. Taking a second derivative of a degenerate fit is exactly how a phantom signal is manufactured. This hypothesis is only admissible after the fit is replaced by a published rebound/viscosity model.

H62-I — Scarp steps, cross-cut gated · rank 3 — screened, and quarantined

  • Layers: lidar_scarp_features_u8.tif bands step_max (3) and cross_max (8), gated by valid (12); 100 m DEM for the contour orientation.
  • Signature: a metre-scale vertical step whose orientation cuts across the local palaeo-contour. |sin(θ_line − θ_contour)| separates a fault crossing the shoreline from a beach berm or delta topset running along it.
  • Result, MEASURED: step_max alone 0.09255 (2.16× control), cross_max alone 0.09524 (2.22× control), the gated combination at 60,000 dots 0.086663 (2.00× control). The gate does not raise credit over the ungated band — an honest negative within a positive result.
  • Why it is quarantined: 87.5 % of its dots fall within 3 px of gemsdoe23-h30-arrangement-matched -habitat and the symmetric Jaccard of the two 3-px halos is 0.4465 (limit 0.25). Both fields are driven by the same LiDAR composite, so this is real lane drift. Logged and stopped.
  • Coverage limit: the LiDAR composite is non-zero over only 31.72 % of the grid, which is the binding constraint on this family's spread — not the geology.

H62-H — Channel-steepness (χ) knickzones · rank 4

  • Layers: 100 m DEM; flow accumulation and the χ transform χ = ∫ (A₀/A)^θ dℓ with θ ≈ 0.45; optionally slope_p90 (band 1) for corroboration.
  • Signature: linear χ anomalies and knickzone clusters — a channel response that integrates displacement over a whole catchment rather than expressing it at one pixel.
  • Why it catches a missing fault: low-slip or blanket-covered scarps leave no mappable trace but do perturb drainage. The catalogue is map-based; χ is not.
  • How it differs: nothing in this repository computes flow routing. The named non-fault mimic is lithology — a knickzone at a contact between resistant and weak rock is indistinguishable from one at a fault without a geologic map, which is why this must be reported with that confuser.
  • Cost: medium. Needs a depression-filled DEM and a χ implementation; both are standard.

H62-F — Pluvial lake-flat scarp habitat · REFUTED, kept as a negative result

  • Layers: 100 m DEM (slope, local relief, elevation relative to the datum).
  • Hypothesis as stated: post-pluvial scarps are best preserved where the pre-fault surface is young, flat and unvegetated — the Lahontan lake plains and deltas — so a hard-core dot field over that habitat should earn credit above chance.
  • Result, MEASURED: 0.00118, i.e. 0.03× the isolated-random control. The most decisively refuted field screened this session, by a factor of ~36 below chance.
  • What it rules out: the "habitat" argument in its simple form. Lake flats are not where the off-catalogue fault population lives — or, more cautiously, the instrument used cannot see them there. The Lahontan datum band itself (1308–1360 m) also came in below chance at 0.02614 (0.61× control), which is the direct measurement behind this lane's repeated negative verdicts.

What the screening says about the instrument itself — stated, not buried

Every number above comes from SGMC 1:750 k off-catalogue, a public geological compilation. Two facts limit it. First, a compilation is biased toward geomorphology its compilers could already see, so a 1 m LiDAR scarp response is expected to score well against it partly by construction. Second, the same repository records (docs/irregularities.md §4) that catalogue-like proxies anti-select novelty: a Poisson control beat an artifact that scored 0.2778 live. The honest reading of the table is therefore "this is the ranking this instrument gives", not "this is the ranking the organizer gives". Only a submission-page receipt can settle it, and this repository holds none.
