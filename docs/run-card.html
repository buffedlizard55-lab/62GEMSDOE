hypothesisLake Lahontan's ~15 ka shoreline is a near-level datum; where it is stepped in elevation or deflected laterally along strike, a structure younger than the highstand cuts it. Those structures are the class of fault the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue is incomplete for, because they are expressed as basin-margin scarps rather than range crests.
mechanismFit a smooth isobase through the pluvial-lake-margin band of the detrended-elevation surface (6 km Gaussian, so differential rebound between sub-basins is absorbed rather than forced into one plane); flag robust residual outliers (steps, k=2.5 MAD) and abrupt azimuth kinks (>22 deg over 3 px, lateral deflections); seed a trace at each flag and extend it while DEM-ridge evidence (across-strike profile curvature + scarp-band slope + TMI/isostatic-gravity horizontal gradient) stays above 0.60; emit single-pixel dots at >=3 px spacing, capped at the organiser-confirmed optimum budget, with every dot within 3 px of a mapped fault deleted.
named non-fault process that could mimic itDifferential post-pluvial isostatic rebound itself. Rebound is spatially variable across the Lahontan sub-basins, so a purely isostatic warp of the shoreline produces elevation steps and azimuth kinks with no fault at all. Secondary mimics: (a) alluvial-fan toes and debris-flow lobes, which create slope breaks in the same TPI band; (b) the ~12-14 ka Truckee/Carson River channel and delta margins, which are erosional not tectonic; (c) bedrock lithologic contacts expressed as differential erosion. The lane cannot separate (a) from a genuine scarp at 100 m pixels -- that needs the 1 m lidar in 1m_DEM_links.csv, which is not reachable from this sandbox.
