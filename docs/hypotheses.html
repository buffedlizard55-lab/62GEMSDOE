#1 · H62-A — Isobase-residual throw window x basin-margin habitat (IMPLEMENTED) viable

Layersband 12 det_elev, band 19 det_elev_slope, band 3 tmi_hg, band 18 iso_grav_anom_hg
Physical signatureSmooth isobase fit (6 km Gaussian) through the pluvial-lake-margin band; bounded residual window [15,130) m; plan-curvature-selected contour-parallel benches; across-strike profile curvature + horizontal-gradient rank for trace support.
Why a missing faultA fault that offsets a 15 ka shoreline is by construction younger than the highstand, so it is a fault the catalogue is most likely to be missing or mislocated. The measured habitat -- deep topographic position (tpi < -120 m) with a scarp-scale slope -- is 2.3-3.2x enriched, and the throw-window intersection with habitat >= 2.0 measured 3.67x over 66,163 px.
How it differsNo sibling repository fits an isobase or uses a bounded throw window. The closest lanes are gravity basement-depth steps (61GEMSDOE), radiometric scarp fusion (GEMSDOE46) and Euler-deconvolution lineaments (GEMSDOE40) -- all subsurface or potential-field, none shoreline-datum based.
Costimplemented
Expected / measuredHOLDOUT-DTI 0.1224 (CI 0.1154-0.1284) vs random lines 0.0228

#2 · H62-B — Isobase-parallel strike continuity: gaps between collinear mapped traces viable

Layersband 12 det_elev, labels.tif (visible faults only)
Physical signatureFit a RANSAC line to each collinear run of visible catalogue traces inside a basin-margin band, then score the GAP between the ends of two runs whose strikes agree within ~10 deg and whose perpendicular offset is < ~300 m. Emit dots only in the gap.
Why a missing faultPierce & Wesnousky's Smith Valley segments are exactly this: short NW-striking segments in a basin centre that earlier maps missed. A gap between two collinear mapped traces is a fault the catalogue demonstrably failed to connect -- the single most direct definition of 'missing' available without hidden labels.
How it differsGEMSDOE27's topo-gap-closure worked on a d1.5 base but closed gaps in a probability surface, not in the catalogue's own strike geometry.
Cost~3 h; no new data
Expected / measuredmedium-high: targets catalogue incompleteness directly

#3 · H62-C — Radiometric K/Th breach of the clay cap at basin-margin faults data-blocked

LayersGeoDAWN radiometrics (NOT in the 19-band stack) x band 17 cond_surf x band 15 depth_to_base_surf
Physical signaturePotassium metasomatism and hydrothermal clay alteration along a permeable fault produce a local K/Th ratio anomaly that breaches the low-conductivity cap. Target the intersection of high cond_surf, shallow depth_to_base_surf and a radiometric ratio edge.
Why a missing faultThis is the only signature in the list that is diagnostic of the GEOTHERMAL part of 'geothermal-indicative fault' rather than of a fault per se, which is what the prize is actually about.
How it differsGEMSDOE46 fused a radiometric scarp detector but as a scarp proxy, not as a K/Th alteration ratio against a clay cap.
CostBLOCKED on data: the radiometric channels are not among the 19 bands. The free official source is the USGS GeoDAWN release (https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and) and the ScienceBase item 657e1d85d34e23d3533209f7. Public domain, but usgs.gov is not in this sandbox's egress allowlist -- OBTAINABILITY UNVERIFIED HERE.
Expected / measuredhigh if obtained; cannot be validated without the radiometric grid

#4 · H62-D — Strain-rate dilatation bands as the deep expression of basin-margin faults viable

Layersband 7 geod_shearrate, band 8 geod_dilaterate, band 4 geod_2ndinv
Physical signatureDilatation-rate maxima aligned along strike with a basin-margin scarp. A locked or creeping fault shows as a narrow band of dilatation contrast in GPS/InSAR-derived strain even where no surface expression is mapped.
Why a missing faultStrain rate is the one layer family that is insensitive to erosion and alluviation, so it can localise a fault whose surface expression has been buried -- the failure mode that makes catalogue faults incomplete in basin centres.
How it differs61GEMSDOE's H61-M used a gravity+magnetic conjunction; no sibling lane uses the geodetic strain family as the primary localiser.
Cost~2 h; data already in the stack
Expected / measuredlow-medium: the 100 m grid smooths strain heavily

#5 · H62-E — Earthquake-density azimuthal anisotropy (deq/ieq directional decomposition) viable

Layersband 10 deq_n100a15, band 16 ieq_n100a15
Physical signatureThese bands are already azimuthally parameterised (n=100 km radius, a=15 deg azimuth). The ratio between two azimuthal samples isolates lineations; a fault shows as a persistent azimuthal contrast rather than a radial one.
Why a missing faultSeismicity clusters on active structures, and an unmapped active fault is precisely the target class.
How it differsNo sibling lane decomposes these bands; all treat them as scalar distance/intensity.
Cost~2 h; data already in the stack
Expected / measuredlow: the parameterisation is not documented well enough to invert confidently

The ranking rule actually applied

H62-A was implemented and validated on the spatially-blocked holdout before any slot was considered, per the brief. H62-B is next by expected value per hour and needs no new data. H62-C has the highest ceiling but is data-blocked: the GeoDAWN radiometric channels are not among the 19 provided bands, so the free official source (the USGS GeoDAWN release) is named and its obtainability from this sandbox is recorded as unverified rather than assumed. H62-D and H62-E use data already in hand and are cheap, but their expected gains are lower and are ranked below accordingly.
