H62-M — Base-level isobase of the pluvial depositional datum · rank 1, implemented

  • Layers. geodawn_dem_a2_100m.tif (absolute elevation, 100 m, EPSG:32611 — the layer IR-62-B-09 says every previous lane lacked); topo_10m_derived_100m_u8.tif bands slope_p90 (1), relief_local (5), curv_prof_absmax (6), hs_lineament (8), dem_mean (9); the competition catalogue only as a mask.
  • Signature. Not the DEM itself but its base-level surface — the morphological floor of the topography (a running minimum followed by a smoothing, i.e. the level of the local depositional plain). In a pluvial-lake basin that surface is the lake floor and its shoreline bench system; its height above the local base level (HAB) is the geomorphic position of the palaeo-shoreline. The isobase is a σ = 6 km smooth fit of that base-level surface, which absorbs waterplane tilt and post-pluvial rebound; the flags are (a) robust residual steps (|r| > k·MAD) and (b) azimuth kinks of the base-level contour (|Δθ| over 3 px). Each flag seeds a trace that is extended while multi-scale ridge evidence holds.
  • Why it should catch a fault the catalogue lacks. A step in a base-level surface whose depositional age is latest Pleistocene is, by construction, deformation younger than that surface. The catalogue is compiled from range-front scarps, field mapping and published Quaternary-fault studies; the population it systematically misses is basin-floor and basin-margin scarps in young lacustrine and playa deposits, where the offset is metres, the scarp is degraded, and there is no bedrock expression. That is precisely what Pierce & Wesnousky (SCEC poster, 2016) reported in Smith Valley: two previously unmapped northwest-striking segments in the basin centre offsetting Holocene sediments. Briggs & Wesnousky (JGR, 2004) and Dong et al. (Geosphere) are the type examples of the same population at Pyramid Lake and outboard of the Wassuk Range front.
  • How it differs from everything already implemented. Three concrete differences, each verifiable in the code: (i) every previous lane fit its isobase on det_elev — a detrended band whose long-wavelength content was removed by someone else's operator; (ii) none derived a base-level (morphological floor) surface at all — the datum was either the raw elevation band or a TPI of it; (iii) previous lanes confined the datum band to Lake Lahontan's type area or to a fixed elevation window (1308–1360 m, which measured 0.61× chance), whereas HAB is defined per basin and therefore covers every pluvial basin in the footprint (Lahontan sub-basins, Carson Sink, Smoke Creek–Black Rock, Winnemucca, Humboldt, Walker).
  • Named non-fault process that could mimic it. Differential post-pluvial isostatic rebound itself: rebound is spatially variable between sub-basins, so a purely isostatic warp produces both elevation steps and azimuth kinks in the datum with no fault at all. Secondary mimics: alluvial-fan toes and debris-flow lobes (slope breaks in the same TPI band); the Truckee/Carson/Walker river and delta margins of ~12–14 ka, which are erosional; and bedrock lithologic contacts expressed as differential erosion. The 6 km isobase is the control for the first; the HAB band and the ridge-evidence requirement are the controls for the others.

H62-Q — Multi-physics corroboration of the same seeds · rank 2, implemented as a re-rank

  • Layers. training_features.tif bands tmi_hg (3, total magnetic intensity horizontal gradient), iso_grav_anom_hg (18, isostatic gravity horizontal gradient), rtp (2, reduced to pole), geod_2ndinv (4, geodetic second invariant of the strain-rate tensor), geod_shearrate (7), depth_to_base_surf (15).
  • Signature. A datum step that is also on a geophysical lineament of the same strike: the product of the datum-step evidence with an oriented lineament response on the potential-field gradients, evaluated at the local strike of the flag.
  • Why it should catch a missing fault. A geomorphic step alone can be a fan toe or a beach berm. A step that coincides with a subsurface density or magnetization contrast is a structure. Structures with a geophysical expression but a sub-metre or buried surface scarp are exactly the class an outcrop- and imagery-based catalogue misses.
  • How it differs. The lanes that used geophysics used it as an independent detector (ridge/lineament consensus). Here it is used strictly as a corroborator of a seed the datum already produced — it can only re-rank or suppress H62-M's traces, never create one.
  • Leakage risk, stated before the result. geod_2ndinv, geod_shearrate and geod_dilaterate are derived from GNSS/InSAR strain rates, and strain rate localises on known, active faults; deq_n100a15/ieq_n100a15 are earthquake-distance and density fields. All five are therefore plausible catalogue proxies and are tested on the leakage canary before being trusted. The potential-field gradients (3, 18, 2) are not.

H62-P — Published rebound model as the isobase prior · rank 3, deferred

Replace the free smooth isobase with (a) a plane plus (b) the published post-Lahontan rebound deflection field (Adams, Wesnousky & Bills, 1999, attribute ~22 m of post-highstand deflection to rebound with a small down-to-the-north tilt), so that the residual is interpretable in metres of fault offset rather than in arbitrary detrending units. This is the direct cure for IR-62-03 (the isobase fit is degenerate in its linear part). Not implemented this session because the published field is a figure and a set of station elevations, not a downloadable raster, and digitising it by hand would introduce exactly the kind of unverified number this repository exists to avoid. Named source needed: the station/ shoreline-elevation tables in Adams, Wesnousky & Bills (1999) and the USGS/GSA data repository that accompanies them.

H62-N — Drainage deflection across the pluvial datum · rank 4, deferred

Channels that cross a post-datum fault are deflected or beheaded. The signature is a systematic azimuth anomaly of the drainage network restricted to the HAB band, measured after flow routing on the filled DEM. Nothing in this repository computes flow routing, so it is genuinely new; the cost is a depression-fill and a D8 router over 12.3 M cells, and the named confuser is lithology (a knickzone or deflection at a resistant/weak contact is indistinguishable from one at a fault without a geologic map). Deferred on cost, and because the SGMC screening instrument cannot see channel deflection at 1:750 k.

H62-R — Bench-width asymmetry across the datum contour · rank 5, deferred

On a faulted shoreline the bench is wider on the downthrown side, so bench width is asymmetric across the datum contour. It is a genuinely different observable from a step. Deferred because it shares H62-I's input (the LiDAR step_max composite) and H62-I was quarantined for duplicate-drift against gemsdoe23-h30-arrangement-matched-habitat (87.5 % of dots within 3 px); any new hypothesis built on that composite inherits the tripwire and would have to be justified against it.

Screening protocol for this session

  1. Instrument. Mean per-dot 300 m kernel credit against SGMC-minus-catalogue, catalogue +3 px masked (Experiment 1). Bar: the hard-core Poisson control 0.1190; references: h33 0.1324 (0.2778 OWNER-REPORTED), H62-A 0.1347.
  2. Null. A Poisson hard-core (300 m) field of the same mass, drawn from the same habitat mask the candidate is allowed to use — not a uniform-random field — so that a "habitat" hypothesis cannot pass by occupying the right geology.
  3. Holdout. The protocol's hide-and-recover holdout is still run and reported, labelled HOLDOUT-DTI, with the leakage canary; but per IR-62-B-11 it is not used to promote or reject.
  4. Budget. Three experiments, then stop: E1 instrument calibration (done), E2 hypothesis screening, E3 mass calibration and the shipped artifact.
