Two facts already separate it from anything this session produced: it is 23× denser than the 1,660-dot datum lane, and it is basin-wide rather than confined to one sub-region.

What generated it [SIBLING-MEASURED, read from that repository's own research note and code]: a multi-physics oriented-lineament consensus — a scale-normalised Hessian line response (Frangi/Sato, both polarities) at σ = 1.2 and 2.5 px on rtp, tmi, mag_anom, iso_grav_anom, det_elev, cond_surf, depth_to_base_surf, the GeoDAWN radiometrics and the 1 m LiDAR scarp composite, multiplied by a second-order orientation order parameter R = |Σ_p w_p e^{2iθ_p}| / Σ_p w_p, so a pixel scores only when independent physics agree that a line exists and agree on its strike.

2. Why that scores 0.2778 — the arithmetic, not the narrative

The published metric is DTI = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2·FP_w + 0.8·FN_w + ε) with a 300 m triangular kernel [OFFICIAL, competition page 967]. Writing T = TP_w, S = FP_w, N = T + FN_w (the total kernel-weighted truth mass, so FN_w = N − T):

DTI = T / (0.2·(T + S) + 0.8·N)

Three consequences follow immediately, and they are the explanation:

(a) DTI is strictly decreasing in S, so a score of 0.2778 certifies a floor on recall. Setting S = 0 gives the best case, hence

T/N  >=  0.8·DTI / (1 − 0.2·DTI)  =  0.8(0.2778)/(1 − 0.2(0.2778))  =  0.2353

**The artifact demonstrably found at least 23.5 % (kernel-weighted) of a truth set that is explicitly defined as the faults absent from the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue** — and any false positives it carries push the required recall above that. This is the strongest true statement available about it, and no local instrument in this repository can produce it, because the only local truth is the catalogue. That asymmetry is the whole reason the leaderboard exists.

(b) The metric is recall-dominated, and the marginal bar for adding a dot is low. Adding one dot that earns p of truth credit and 1 − p of penalty moves T → T + p, S → S + (1 − p), i.e. the denominator by exactly 0.2. The score rises iff

p  >  0.2 · DTI          →   for this artifact:  p > 0.0556

Any dot with better than a 5.6 % chance of landing within 300 m of a hidden new fault raises the score. That is a low bar, and it is the quantitative reason a 37,654-dot basin-wide field is a rational design rather than spam — and also why it is very likely still under-filled.

(c) The mechanism justifies high recall by construction. Per the organizers, a "new fault" includes newly mapped geometry of existing systems [OFFICIAL, thread 11536]. A consensus field that fires along whole lineament systems inherits the recall of every mapped system and extends it into the unmapped continuations — exactly the population the test set is drawn from. Dense and basin-wide is not a bug here; it is the mechanism working.

3. Can a higher-scoring submission be generated? Yes — and not by a better holdout

Ranked by expected gain per unit cost, with the reasoning above as the foundation:

  1. Fill the density up to the marginal bar. At any plausible operating point the bar is ~0.01–0.06 truth-credit per dot, while a structured lineament field earns far more than that near mapped systems. Sweeping density against metric.marginal_bar is hours of work and needs no new geology. This is the single highest-EV action available to the group.
  2. Raise p by adding physics that agree on the same lines — not more dots, better dots. The untried candidates are ranked in hypotheses.md; the pattern that worked (several independent fields agreeing on a strike) is the pattern to extend, because each extra agreeing field raises p without raising S proportionally.
  3. Do not optimise the local catalogue proxy. It is measured to anti-select novelty in two independent repositories (ρ = +0.087 versus live; Poisson 0.0669 > h33 0.0067 here). Optimising it walks the group away from the 0.2778 design.
  4. Change one factor per weekly experiment. p is unobservable locally, so a submission that changes both density and detector cannot be interpreted afterwards. The submission page is the only ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED channel that exists; treat each slot as an experiment with one variable.

4. What this says about the file this session delivered

The datum-offset lane's 1,660 dots sit 23× below the density of the artifact that scored 0.2778, and 1,641 of them fall in a single 171 × 110 km sub-region. Even if those dots individually cleared the marginal bar — which is unknowable locally — their coverage bounds what they can add. That is why the verdict on the download page is "legal, unique, low-risk Phase-1 novelty probe, not the lead horse", and why the ranked next step (§3.1) is density calibration rather than more detector work.

5. The claim this document does not make

It does not claim 0.2778 is reproducible, or that any file here is comparable to it. The recall floor in §2(a) is a necessary condition implied by the owner-reported score, not a measurement of that artifact made here; the artifact's own truth (the hidden test faults) is not available in this sandbox, by the organizers' explicit policy [OFFICIAL, thread 11527].
