0. The metric, transcribed and cross-checked

From the organiser's problem-description page:

k(d) = max(1 − d/R, 0)                     R = 300 m = 3 px at 100 m

TP_w = Σ_{g∈G} max_{x : d(x,g) ≤ R} p(x)·k(d(x,g))
FP_w = Σ_{x : p(x)>0} p(x)·[1 − max_{g∈G} k(d(x,g))]
FN_w = Σ_{g∈G} [1 − max_{x : d(x,g) ≤ R} p(x)·k(d(x,g))]

DTI(α,β) = TP_w / (TP_w + α·FP_w + β·FN_w + ε)      α = 0.2, β = 0.8

Three independent checks in tests/test_gems62.py: the organiser's worked example (TPw=3.00, FPw=1.89, FNw=2.00 → 0.60); agreement of the fast vectorised implementation with a literal O(|G||P|) brute-force transcription of the three sums on random grids; and the algebraic identity DTI = T/(0.2(T+F)+0.8|G|) together with FN_w = |G| − TP_w. The reference solution's TverskyLoss(alpha=0.2, beta=0.8, mode="binary") independently confirms the parameters.

2. The lane, step by step

2.1 Pluvial-lake-margin band

Topographic position index TPI = det_elev − smooth(det_elev, σ=25 px = 2.5 km), NaN-weighted so the footprint edge does not leak. The margin band is TPI ∈ [-400.0, -40.0) m and slope ∈ [1.0, 30.0]° → 1,177,085 px (22.8% of the footprint). A Gaussian weight floor of 0.35 marks the pixels whose TPI is trustworthy; the rest are excluded rather than filled.

2.2 The isobase fit

isobase = smooth(det_elev restricted to the margin band, σ=60.0 px = 6 km). A local smooth fit rather than one global plane, because post-pluvial rebound is differential: the datum tilts between the Lahontan sub-basins (Pyramid Lake / Winnemucca / Lahontan Reservoir / Walker / Carson Sink / Smith Valley), so a single plane over a 330 × 373 km footprint would be geologically wrong. 6 km is long enough to be a datum and short enough to follow rebound between sub-basins.

2.3 Benches

bench = margin ∧ slope ∈ [3,22]° ∧ |plan curvature| ≤ its 55th percentile in-band → 375,991 px. Plan curvature is the curvature of the contour line: near zero means straight-to-gently-arcuate, which is what a wave-cut bench is, and it rejects gullies, fan heads and ridge crests. IR-62-BENCH-01: an earlier version also required the residual to be small, which is circular and produced zero flags; the residual test now lives only in the step flag.

2.4 Flagging steps — a bounded throw window

step = bench ∧ |resid − median| ∈ [15.0, 130.0) m → 271,102 px. The window is measured, not chosen: catalogue enrichment by |isobase residual| inside the bench band is 2.26–2.77× below 210 m, then 1.45× (210–260), 1.16× (260–320), 0.66× (320–400) and 0.00× above 400 m (403 px, not one mapped fault). An unbounded outlier test keeps exactly the depleted tail. The bounds also have an independent physical meaning: 1–8 mm/yr over 15 kyr is 15–120 m of throw, which brackets the slow-to-fast range of Great Basin faults.

2.5 Flagging lateral deflections

Azimuth kinks: |Δstrike| > 22.0° over 3 px, computed in the double-angle representation so the [0,π) fold of a strike is handled as a proper circle → 1,173 px. IR-62-DEFL-01: this flag measured 1.12× enrichment, i.e. no signal at 100 m. It is kept because the lane specifies it and reported as a negative rather than dropped.

2.6 Seeding and extending traces

Each flag seeds a trace that walks both ways along the local isobase strike while DEM-ridge evidence stays above 0.55. Evidence = 0.65·(0.60·rank|profile curvature| + 0.40·rank(sclope in [3,25]°)) + 0.35·(rank TMI-HG, rank isostatic-gravity HG), all rank-normalised inside the footprint so the combination is scale-free. Result: 200,181 traces, 595,434 px, median length 1.0 px, mean 3.89, p90 10.0, max 160. IR-62-EXT-01: the first version derived the walk direction from the evidence gradient and produced median length 1 px — the traces never extended. Direction now comes from the isobase strike, which is well posed; evidence is only the stopping criterion, which is what the lane text says.

2.7 Habitat weighting from visible faults only

A 9×8 binning of (TPI, slope) gives a smoothed per-pixel enrichment weight (Laplace prior count 40 so sparse bins cannot fabricate a rate). Under the holdout the weights are rebuilt from the visible faults only, per fold — never from the withheld ones.

2.8 Emission

Greedy non-maximum suppression in priority order at ≥3 px separation, capped at the budget, then every dot within 3.0 px of a mapped catalogue fault is deleted. Values are strictly binary {0.0, 1.0}; outside the footprint 0.0; no nodata tag.

The physically coherent reading: the most fault-rich terrain in this footprint is deep topographic position with a scarp-scale slope — the basin-side toe of a range front. That is exactly where a pluvial-lake shoreline is cut, and exactly where a fault that offsets it will show. Range crests (TPI > +120 m) are depleted to 0.28–0.34×, which is why a detector that ranks by "roughness" or by residual magnitude alone loses.
