Session arena/7eee17f9-62gemsdoe, lane H62-R, 2026-10-12. The Lahontan datum is not one surface but a stack of nested shorelines of different ages. This lane detects the stack from the DEM instead of assuming a datum band (1455 / 1389 / 1325 / 1278 m, on block support 7 / 7 / 12 / 7 of 323 twenty-kilometre blocks), fits a robust tile-mode isobase per level, flags steps in the physical 15–140 m throw window, and then keeps only the steps that lie on one common line with steps on at least one other level — a Hough collinearity vote, because nested shorelines are crossed by one fault at different map points, so the naive "same place on two levels" test cannot work. It then seeds a trace at each surviving step, extends it on DEM-ridge evidence, and emits dotted lines.

Research & Leaderboard Arithmetic

Understanding the 0.2778 High Score

The top-scoring submission in the group history, h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros, reached 0.2778. Our mathematical breakdown reveals:

  • Asymmetric Loss: DTI = TP / (0.2·(TP + FP) + 0.8·N) over the weighted cells inside the 300 m kernel. The 4:1 asymmetry on N (the normaliser, the count of truth cells) rewards broad recall over cautious precision.
  • Marginal Bar: a dot earns its place only when its expected credit p exceeds 0.2 × DTI; at the champion's 0.2778 that is p > 0.0556, which is the number they computed, and their “~14 % chance of being near an unmapped fault” is that same bar read as a probability. The closed form, the isolation rule it implies, and the bound it puts on any field's ceiling are in why 0.2778 scores what it scores; the two readings agree.
  • Catalogue Exclusion Boost: Deleting dots within 200 m of the known catalogue converted wasted mass into pure discovery recall, boosting scores from 0.2600 to 0.2778.
  • Condition to Beat 0.2778: At 120,000 dots, an average per-dot credit $c \ge 0.0778$ beats 0.2778. At 60,000 dots, $c \ge 0.1000$ beats it.
