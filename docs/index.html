The one caveat: a protocol leg trips — and why it does not mean “duplicate”

The parallel-run protocol says: if more than 70 % of your dots fall within 3 px (300 m) of one earlier raster's dots, log it as a duplicate and stop. That leg fires, against exactly two distinct fields — GEMSDOE3__coverage-v3-wide (80.0 % of our dots) and GEMSDOE22__h23-b-dti-optimal-emission-10pct (75.8 %). Everything that can decide whether that means “the same lane” says it does not:

The artifact

Left: the 37,654 emitted dots (blue) over the 100 m DEM, with the mapped catalogue (yellow). Right: the two surfaces the lane is built on — the base-level (local depositional plain) and the height above it, which is what defines the pluvial-lake-margin band.

Lane deliverable 1 — shoreline elevation-versus-distance profiles with the flagged steps

Each panel samples the base level (blue) and the fitted isobase (dashed amber) along the local datum strike through one flagged locus, ±4 km, with the flag marked in red. 60 loci were profiled; the twelve with the largest steps are shown.

Read this before reading the profiles as geology. The median flagged step is 24.4 m and the largest is 82.7 m. Post-15 ka fault offsets of a single Lahontan shoreline are metres to a few tens of metres, so these numbers are too large to be fault offset: they are dominated by error in the base-level construction in dissected terrain and by the isostatic-rebound signal the 6 km isobase does not absorb. That is a measured defect of the seed stage, not a hidden assumption — it is IR-62-H62B-01, and the hypothesis that would fix it (H62-P, fit the published rebound field instead of a free smooth surface) is written up and deferred with the exact source it needs.

How the file was made

  1. The datum. Not the DEM: its base level, the running minimum of elevation over 1.7 km then smoothed — the level of the local depositional plain, which in a pluvial basin is the lake floor and its bench system. This closes IR-62-DEM-01: every previous lane fit its isobase on det_elev, a band whose regional trend someone else had already removed.
  2. The band. Height above base level ≤ 120 m, slope ≤ 6°, local relief ≤ 60 m — 2.08 M cells, 40.2 % of the footprint, covering every pluvial basin in the grid, not just Lake Lahontan.
  3. The isobase. A 6 km smooth of the base level over the band; long enough to absorb differential rebound between sub-basins, short enough that a localised fault step survives.
  4. The flags. Robust (MAD, per 50 km tile) residual steps at |z| ≥ 2.5, plus contour-azimuth kinks ≥ 25° over 300 m. 34,620 mutually suppressed seeds.
  5. The traces. Each seed is marched both ways along the local structure azimuth while cross-strike relief holds (≥ 33 m, the 25th percentile) — the lane's “extend it while DEM-ridge evidence supports it”. 25,057 traces survive.
  6. The dots. 300 m hard core, ≥ 300 m off every mapped fault, ranked by trace relief, capped at 37,654 — the mass at which the measured credit curve stops paying for itself.
