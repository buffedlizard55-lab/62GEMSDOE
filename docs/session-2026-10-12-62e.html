Model projection — a projection is never a score. With the published metric's closed form for isolated unit dots, DTI = c / (0.2 + 0.8·K/M), and the per-dot credit c predicted by experiment 8's leave-artifact-out reward model (c = 0.1044, CV ρ 0.7885, CV RMSE 0.0157), the arithmetic gives 0.2374 at K = 12,000, 0.2204 at K = 13,688 and 0.2826 at K = 8,479. To beat 0.2778 at M = 40,000 a field needs c ≥ 0.1222; to reach the stated target 0.3195 it needs c ≥ 0.1406; the top-5 cut-off 0.3333 needs c ≥ 0.1467. Why no local instrument can certify placement quality

The counterexample that ends the argument. h34-scatter-q50-arr-matched has the champion's exact mass (37,654), a higher credit against the one off-catalogue compilation available here (0.0662 vs 0.0655) and a higher LiDAR-scarp mean (109 vs 82.9) — and it scored 0.0778 against the champion's 0.2778. Every aggregate property this sandbox can measure matched or beat the champion, and the score was 3.6× worse. So:

  • the reward model ranks families, not fields (CV ρ 0.7885 on implied credit, 0.7867 on score);
  • the vote surrogate measures incumbent similarity, which is exactly what the uniqueness protocol forbids maximising (this field reads 0.26× the champion on it, and that is a statement about novelty, not quality);
  • the mandated segmented holdout is structurally blind to this design — with a 300 m catalogue standoff every dot is outside the withheld catalogue positives, so pooled HOLDOUT-DTI is 0.0000 while its own random-dot control scores 0.0575 and its random-line control 0.0170 (2297 segments, 81,993 withheld positives, α 0.2 β 0.8, 300 m triangular kernel, evaluator gems62.holdout_segmented v1). It rewards catalogue hugging, which the leaderboard punishes (IR-62-HOLDOUT-01).

Consequence for the weekly slot: the correct decision is to spend nothing. The field is published because the brief requires a downloadable, obviously-labelled artifact, and because the recipe and the negative results are worth more than another blind probe.
