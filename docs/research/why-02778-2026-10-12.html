1. The metric, verbatim, and the identity that follows from it

OFFICIAL —

competition page 967, §Performance metric,

opened 2026-10-12. Quoted exactly:

> * \(k(d) = (1 - d/R)_+ = \max(1 - d/R,\,0)\), range \(R = 300\) metres

> * \(TP_w = \sum_{g \in G} \max_{x:\,d(x,g) \le R} p(x)\,k(d(x,g))\)

> * \(FP_w = \sum_{x:\,p(x) > 0} p(x)\,\bigl[1 - \max_{g \in G} k(d(x,g))\bigr]\)

> * \(FN_w = \sum_{g \in G} \bigl[1 - \max_{x:\,d(x,g) \le R} p(x)k(d(x,g))\bigr]\)

> * \(DTI(\alpha,\beta) = TP_w / (TP_w + \alpha FP_w + \beta FN_w + \epsilon)\), with

> \(\alpha = 0.2\), \(\beta = 0.8\)

> * worked example: \(TP_w = 3.00\), \(FP_w = 1.89\), \(FN_w = 2.00\) →

> \(3.00/(3.00 + 0.2\cdot1.89 + 0.8\cdot2.00) = 0.60\)

The third line telescopes: \(FN_w = |G| - TP_w\) exactly. Writing \(T = TP_w\),

\(F = FP_w\), \(K = |G|\):

$$

\boxed{\;DTI \;=\; \frac{T}{0.2\,(T+F) \;+\; 0.8K}\;}

\tag{1}

$$

(1) is the whole game. Three consequences:

(a) One identity worth more than any model. Add one unit dot whose realised kernel

weight on the truth set is \(k\) and whose own kernel-to-truth is \(q\).

Then \(\Delta T = k\), \(\Delta F = 1-q\), \(\Delta FN = -k\), so

\(\Delta D = 0.2(\Delta T + \Delta F) + 0.8\Delta FN = 0.2(1-q) - 0.6k\).

Hence \(d(DTI) > 0\) if and only if

$$

k \;>\; \frac{0.2\,DTI}{1 + 0.8\,DTI}.

\tag{2}

$$

At \(DTI = 0.2778\) the bar is \(k > 0.0455\), i.e. the dot must lie within 286 m of a

hidden truth pixel to be worth adding. At the current leaderboard top (0.4275) the bar is

\(k > 0.0694\), i.e. 279 m.

MEASURED refinement (IR-62-11C-01). src/gems62/metric.py::marginal_bar states the

bar as \(k > \alpha\,DTI\) (0.0556 at 0.2778) and its docstring justifies that by

"\(dD = \alpha p\) regardless of \(k\)". That is not right: \(dD = \alpha(\Delta T + \Delta

F) + \beta\,\Delta FN\), and the \(\beta\,\Delta FN\) term is \(-0.8k\), so

\(dD = p(\alpha - \beta k)\), not \(\alpha p\). The two spellings give the same *practical*

conclusion (283 m vs 286 m, a 1 % difference in radius and no change of sign anywhere), but

the identity as written is wrong, and a wrong identity is the kind of thing that later gets

used to justify a large mass. The corrected form (2) is now the one quoted everywhere in

this session's documents.

(b) Exact closed form for isolated unit dots. For \(M\) dots no two of which share a

kernel (nearest-neighbour spacing \(\ge R\)), each dot contributes \(k\) to \(T\) and

\(1-k\) to \(F\), so \(T + F = M\) exactly and (1) becomes

$$

DTI \;=\; \frac{c}{0.2 \;+\; 0.8K/M},

\qquad c = \text{mean per-dot credit}.

\tag{3}

$$

This is the form every design decision in this repository should be quoted against.

\(K > 0\) means mass matters even at constant credit, and \(c\) is the only quality

term.

(c) The ceiling. \(T \le K\), so \(DTI \le K/(0.2K + 0.8K) = 1\) attained only by a

perfect detector. With a hit rate \(p\) (fraction of dots within \(R\) of truth) and mean

credit \(\bar t\) per hit, \(c = p\bar t\), and full recall needs

\(M \ge K/(p\bar t)\).

6. Can a submission beat 0.2778? The honest answer, with the exact condition

**Yes, it is arithmetically possible — and this session did not achieve it, and no field in

this repository has ever been shown to.**

Beating 0.2778 requires, at mass \(M\), a true per-dot credit

$$

c^\star \;>\; 0.2778\,\bigl(0.2 + 0.8K/M\bigr).

$$

At \(K = 12{,}000\) that is \(c^\star > 0.1264\) at 37,654 dots, \(0.0778\) at 120,000,

\(0.0663\) at 240,000. The bar is *inside* reach of the champion's own measured quality —

which is exactly why the leaderboard is beatable — but the repository has not once shown a

field that clears it on any instrument.

Three concrete, checkable requirements, in the order they bind:

  1. Preserve credit through 3–6× more mass. The only experiment in this repository that

would settle it is the paired one specified as H62-N: the *same* detector at 40 k and

120 k dots, submitted on the same day, so that the leaderboard reports

\(dc/dM\) directly. MEASURED at the per-dot level the repository already knows what

that costs: 37,654 → 120,000 dots at *falling* credit takes the champion's 0.2778 down,

not up, and the dotted family shows exactly that inversion.

  1. **Do not spend mass on the mapped catalogue and do not waste it on isolated-random

ground. The champion spends 0 %** of its dots on the catalogue and its median dot is

1,965 m from it. Anything on the catalogue cannot earn credit at all — organizer

clarification, thread 11516 — and it still costs 0.2 of denominator per dot.

  1. Get one receipt. There is still no organizer-confirmed number anywhere in this

repository (organizer_score = null everywhere). Every ranking above is a proxy

ranking, and §5 shows what proxies are worth (1.45× vs 3.8× is the same data measured

two ways).

7. What this session measured that closes the lane's open question

The lane asked whether Lahontan shorelines work as a strain gauge for *this* competition.

The repository's standing limitation said the question could not be answered:

*"Either the band is not fault-rich, or a 1:750 k compilation cannot see 2 m datum

offsets. The two cannot be separated without the organizer's truth."*

They can be separated, and this session separated them with the correct null — a

uniform random point drawn from the same published datum band, not from the whole

footprint. Full receipts: evidence/lane11c_null_test.json.

Reading. The loci are not merely uninformative: pooled, they are **worse than a random

point on the same datum surface**, and they are *anti-selected* for mapped faults by 1.8×.

So the failure is not only "a 1:750 k compilation cannot see 2 m offsets". The

detector's chosen locations are systematically not where faults are — which is what one

should expect geologically: the pluvial-lake margin of the Lahontan basin is a **low-relief

depositional and shorezone surface**, mostly without bedrock faults cropping out, and

Adams, Wesnousky & Bills (1999) put its tectonic signal *at the resolution floor*

("the resolution in defining the elevation of the high shoreline over a broad area is

limited to about 3 m … almost all of the post-Sehoo vertical displacements … are less than

or equal to about 3 m").

**A second refutation, this session's novel hypothesis (H62-R) — multi-datum

corroboration is refuted at 100 m.** The idea was sound: a fault offsets *every* level

datum that crosses it, so requiring the same step on two independent datums (Sehoo ≈ 1332 m,

Younger Dryas ≈ 1230 m, late Holocene ≈ 1204 m) should filter every non-tectonic confuser.

MEASURED: the observed rate at which an independent datum reproduces a step line is

0.081 / 0.076 / 0.213 against a random-strike null of 0.082 / 0.067 / 0.174 —

enrichment 0.99× / 1.13× / 1.22×, p = 0.63 / 0.33 / 0.28. The filter carries no

signal. Reported as a negative result; that is a deliverable, not a failure.
