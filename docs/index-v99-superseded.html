The question

Why did the 0.2778 file score highest, and can we beat it? The 0.2778 artifact is the family's 0.2708 emission (40,199 dots) with every dot within 200 m of the mapped catalogue deleted (2,545 dots removed, 37,654 left). For unit-valued, non-overlapping dots the published index reduces to

DTI = c·N / (α·N + β·|G|)          c = mean kernel credit per dot, N = emitted mass, |G| = truth mass

Deleting a dot that earns no credit lowers N while leaving the pooled credit T unchanged, so DTI rises mechanically. That is a precision edit, not a discovery. The same algebra inverts the two owner-recorded rows of the family to give c ≈ 0.122 and |G| ≈ 12,000 cells — and it reproduces the observed 0.2778 to within 0.02 %:

What the lane did

  1. Datum mapping. 90,664 wave-planed cells inside the published deformed-elevation window (1313.5–1345.8 m; literature range 1318.4–1340.8 m) → 176 benches.
  2. Level separation. The residual histogram is multi-modal (modes at -10.2, -7.2, -4.2, -1.2, 1.8, 4.8 m) — the stacked-shoreline mimic. The dominant strand (176 benches / 60,227 cells) is kept so a Sehoo bench is never contrasted with an Eetza bench.
  3. Isobases. Robust 3rd-order global fit (σ = 12.16 m) for the domal rebound + northward tilt, then a locally robust plane fit on the single strand: σ = 2.99 m, i.e. below the published 3 m elevation-method resolution. The local fit is bounded by two support guards (a gradient cap and a neighbourhood-band clamp) because an unguarded plane fitted 12 km away produced a phantom +25.7 m offset in the first run of this lane.
  4. Flags — and the honest count. 1 step location survives a two-stage test: 43 bench pairs clear the published 3 m resolution gate, but the significance gate — 2.5 × √2 × σ = 12.12 m, with σ = 3.43 m from the median pairwise difference between nearby benches — rejects 42 of them as indistinguishable from beach-crest scatter. The estimator matters: a global MAD is masked by the offset it is meant to detect (a synthetic 12 m step over 20 km inflated it to 6.02 m and rejected itself), so the pairwise median is used and the global MAD (2.99 m → gate 10.57 m) is reported as a cross-check. Both accept the same single location on this data. Those borderline candidates are published worst-first in the run card (step_pair_test.borderline_candidates), because a rejected candidate is as much a result as an accepted one. The survivor is +12.93 m over 2537 m (5.1 m/km) — a gradient no smooth rebound field can absorb. 4 lateral deflection locations (≥ 35° azimuth change) are reported as locations, not as raw pair counts.
  5. Traces. Fault azimuth from the datum geometry, not the local DEM grain: a vertical step means the fault crosses the shoreline (az + 90°), a lateral deflection means it runs along it (the Pyramid Lake fault-zone case). 5 traces survive evidence-gated extension (coherence ≥ 0.15, relief ≥ 4 m), mean length 6,560 m.
  6. Emission. Unit dots at 300 m kernel-matched spacing, ≥ 200 m from the mapped catalogue. 99 dots.

Why the holdout number is unusually trustworthy here, and also does not answer the real question. The lane reads the catalogue for nothing except an exclusion gate that can only remove mass, so withheld positives provably cannot help it — this is a leak-free hide-and-recover, unlike every catalogue-reading method in the family. But the competition's target is exactly the fault population the catalogue lacks and the organizers mask out of scoring, so the number is a lower bound on recovering a mapped population and says nothing about the hidden target. Both facts are stated wherever the number appears.
