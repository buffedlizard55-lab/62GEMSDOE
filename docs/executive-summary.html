The 60-second version

  1. Log in to the competition data tab and accept the rules. Download sample_submission.tif — it defines the grid.
  2. Produce a single-band float32 GeoTIFF that matches the grid exactly: EPSG 32611, 100 m, shape 3730 × 3292, transform (100, 0, 243350, 0, −100, 4508550), values in [0, 1], null/NaN outside the data bounds.
  3. Open the submission form, choose your file, paste a short Note (≤ 140 characters so you can tell submissions apart later), and press Submit. Uploading does not consume a slot until you confirm; a scored submission does.
  4. Budget: up to three scored submissions per week. Spend them deliberately.

1. The file the portal wants — the exact contract

Verbatim from the problem description, “# Submission format”:

  • “same projected coordinate reference system as the training data … EPSG 32611”
  • “same resolution … 100m”
  • “same bounds as the training data, and data outside the bounds is null or nan”
  • “a single layer with datatype of 32-bit float (float32) with values between 0 and 1”

A single-band .tif, or a .zip containing one, is accepted.

2. The error you already hit, and why

“Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]” — this is a range check that sees a value outside [0, 1]. The three common causes, in order:

  1. A float32 nodata sentinel. GeoTIFFs written by GIS tools often carry -3.4028235e38 (“−FLT_MAX”). It is a legitimate nodata marker but it is far outside [0, 1], and if the portal reads it as data the check fails. Fix: write nodata=None, or use NaN.
  2. A NaN nodata tag with NaN cells counted as data. NaN fails any all(v >= 0 and v <= 1) test. Five of the sibling repositories' artifacts are NaN-outside; they are exactly the files that produced this error for the owner.
  3. Genuine out-of-range data — an unnormalised probability or a scaled integer.

The safe pattern used here: write every cell outside the survey footprint as a finite 0.0 with nodata=None, and every finite value in the footprint in [0, 1]. No sentinel, no NaN, nothing for a range check to trip on. This repository ships both encodings so you can choose: -zeros.tif (all finite) and -nan.tif (NaN outside, matching the page's wording literally).

4. What to upload instead — the numbers the metric demands

For unit-valued, non-overlapping dots the published index reduces to DTI = c·N / (α·N + β·|G|). Clearing a target therefore has exactly two routes: more mass, or better placement. At a target of 0.3195, with |G| = 12,000:

routerequirementwhat it means in practice
mass at the family's own placement quality46,402 dots at c = 0.130 emit ~46,000 dots, not 37–45 k, while keeping every dot's credit profile unchanged
placement quality at a moderate mass22,536 dots at c = 0.200 ~22,500 dots each averaging within ~240 m of a truth pixel
fixed at 40,000 dotsc ≥ 0.1406 a 15 % improvement in mean credit over the family's 0.122
fixed at 25,000 dotsc ≥ 0.1866 a 53 % improvement in mean credit — the highest-leverage single change

And the rule that decides whether one more dot is worth emitting — profitable iff its credit exceeds α·DTI, i.e. within 281 m of a truth pixel at DTI = 0.3195. Dots beyond 300 m contribute nothing at all and cost α each.

Label: MODEL. The model's own validity check: applied to the two owner-recorded rows of the family it reproduces 0.2778 to within 0.02 %. It is not a score and not a projection written as a score. For reference, a 225-dot file with flawless placement would score 0.0233.

5. The single most important rule the competition states

From the problem description: the test set is faults “not included in the existing USGS fault database”, and after the Initial prize round “an expert panel will use submitted predictions to update fault labels for the full region”, after which the same submissions are rescored:

“Predictions that helped experts identify previously-unmapped faults can score higher here than in the Initial Prize Round.”

Two consequences most entries miss:

  1. The final round rewards defensible placements, because a human panel decides which of your locations become new labels. A blanket of dots that no geologist can confirm adds nothing there.
  2. The initial round rewards mass geometry, because the score is a weighted-count ratio in which every emitted unit costs α = 0.2 and every missed truth cell costs β = 0.8.

Those two pull in opposite directions. The winning play is the one that satisfies both: mass placed on structures an expert can independently confirm.

7. Things that will get a submission rejected or disqualified — not technical

  • Eligibility. The rules restrict entrants to U.S. citizens/permanent residents, U.S.-incorporated entities and U.S. academics, with FFRDC/DOE/FCOC/MFTRP exclusions. Verify before entering.
  • Generative-AI disclosure. Rule §3.2 requires a narrative statement of how generative AI was used, including in submission elements.
  • External data licence. Any external dataset must be licensed for use in the challenge and shareable with the sponsor. USGS 3DEP and the GeoDAWN release are US Government works (public domain); INGENIOUS/GDR 1391 is CC BY 4.0.
  • No automated leaderboard monitoring. DrivenData's Terms of Use prohibit automated monitoring/copying, and manual monitoring without written consent. This repository contains no scraper, no feed and no monitor, and fetches no leaderboard page.
