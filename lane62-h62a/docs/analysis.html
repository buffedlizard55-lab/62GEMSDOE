Why h33-h33-2-b2 scored 0.2778

Because DTI is a budget, not an accuracy score. From the organiser's published formula (α = 0.2, β = 0.8) and the identity FNw = |G| − TPw:

DTI = T / ( 0.2·(T + F) + 0.8·|G| )

Every unit of emitted mass costs 0.2 whether it hits or misses — because a unit that hits contributes k to T and (1−k) to F, and 0.2·(k + 1 − k) = 0.2 exactly. A unit that hits earns at most 1.0. So the denominator rises by exactly 0.2 per emitted pixel, and the question is never "how confident am I" but "does this pixel's expected kernel credit beat 0.2·DTI".

The break-even bar, derived

Adding one unit of mass with realised kernel weight k:

dT = k,   dF = 1 − k,   dD = α·(dT + dF) = α
dDTI > 0  ⟺  k·D > α·T  ⟺  k > α·DTI

At the group's organiser-confirmed best of 0.2778 the bar is k > 0.05556. The kernel weights attainable on the 100 m lattice are exactly:

[1.0, 0.66667, 0.5286, 0.33333, 0.25464, 0.05719]

So a pixel that coincides with a truth pixel (k = 1) or sits one pixel away (k = 0.667) clears the bar comfortably; the (2,2)-diagonal at k = 0.25464 still clears it (0.25464 > 0.05556); a (3,0) pixel at k = 0 does not, and neither does anything further out. That is the whole geometry of the problem: a dot is worth emitting only if it is within 2 pixels of a truth pixel, and the (2,2) corner is the last profitable ring.

Why dots beat surfaces

A filled probability surface emits many pixels that all cover the same truth pixel. The first earns its k; every subsequent one earns nothing extra (TPw takes a max over predictions per truth pixel) and still costs 0.2. Thinning to single pixels at ≥3 px spacing keeps the geometry and deletes the redundancy — the group measured the same field going 0.1922 → 0.2600 when it was dotted.

Why binary beats calibrated

A dot that hits earns p(x)·k, so a soft value of 0.4 earns 0.4·k. A dot that misses costs 0.2·p(x). Writing 1.0 maximises credit on hits and the penalty on misses is bounded at 0.2 — and because the profitable ring is only 2 px wide, the hit rate at the top of a good ranking is high enough that hard 1.0 wins. Every organiser-confirmed file above 0.24 in the ledger is binary.

Why nothing on the mapped catalogue

The hidden test set is a set of faults not in the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue. A dot within 3 px of an already-mapped trace is spending mass where the hidden truth is, by construction, absent. The group's own forensics: the 0.2778 file is the exact set difference of the 0.2600 file (44,090 dots) with the 6,436 dots within 3 px of the mapped catalogue deleted. 44,090 → 0.2600; 37,654 → 0.2778. That single deletion is worth +0.0178.

Can this lane beat 0.2778? The arithmetic, not the mood

Write the score as T / (0.2·P + 0.8·|G|) where P is the emitted mass (for binary dots, P = number of dots) and T the kernel credit collected. At P = 38,000 and a score of 0.2778 the denominator is 7,600 + 0.8·|G| and T = 0.2778·(7,600 + 0.8·|G|). Since T ≤ P = 38,000, that forces |G| ≤ ~1.6×10⁵; and since the 0.2600 → 0.2778 step removed 6,436 dots without losing credit, the credit those dots carried was ≈0, which pins |G| lower still.

The binding constraint on this lane is not the budget, it is geography. The pluvial-lake-margin band is 1177085 px of a 5167373-px footprint (22.8%), and the false-negative term 0.8·|G| is charged over the whole hidden set, not over the band. A file confined to lake margins cannot raise T enough to move that denominator. This is why the lane's own brief says it "buys defensibility more than area", and it is why the run card's verdict is computed rather than asserted.

What the lane can do is raise the credit density at the top of the ranking. The measured habitat intersection — bench pixels inside the physical throw window with habitat enrichment ≥ 2.0 — carries 3.67× the footprint-mean fault rate over 66,163 px. If the hidden set is distributed like the visible catalogue in that habitat, the top of this ranking is denser in truth than the top of a stack-wide ranking. That is the only route to a higher score that does not require data nobody has, and it is stated as a hypothesis, not a result.
