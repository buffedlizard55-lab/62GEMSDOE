Research & hypotheses

Five candidate geological hypotheses, each naming the layers, the physical signature, why it should catch a fault the catalogue misses, and how it differs from anything already implemented — ranked by expected metric improvement and implementation cost. None is validated unless the evidence column says so.

The lane's mechanism, and its ceiling

The Sehoo highstand shoreline is a near-level datum (15,475 ± 720 cal yr B.P.). Its present elevation about a smooth isobase is a strain measurement, and a fault that breaks it leaves a step or a lateral deflection. Verified literature, with links on Sources:

  • Briggs & Wesnousky 2004, JGR 109 B08402: the Pyramid Lake fault zone offsets post-Lahontan surfaces, ≥4 surface ruptures since desiccation, 2.6 ± 0.3 mm/yr right-lateral.
  • Dong et al. 2014, Geosphere 10(1) 40–48: a strike-slip fault outboard of the Wassuk Range front right-laterally displaces Lahontan recessional shorelines 13.5–13.9 m.
  • Pierce, Wesnousky & Owen 2016, SCEC poster #097: lidar reveals two previously unmapped NW-striking fault segments near the centre of Smith Valley that offset Holocene sediments.
  • Adams, Wesnousky & Bills 1999; Adams et al. 2008: the highstand shoreline runs ~1,340 m near the basin centre to ~1,318 m at the northern edge — post-pluvial isostatic rebound and northward tilting.

The ceiling is structural, not an implementation defect. The faults that offset the Lahontan shoreline are largely the ones already mapped, and the organizer masks catalogue pixels out of scoring entirely. So the lane's honest scope is the handful of unmapped breaks, exactly as its brief anticipated ("if only a handful are convincing, emit only those"). This session measured that handful and found it does not carry a leaderboard score.

Why the top-ranked candidate was not built here

The parallel-run protocol caps this session at 3 experiments. They were: (1) the lane's own product and its holdout, (2) the selectivity sweep, (3) the emission economics. H62-B is the highest-value untested idea and is handed forward with the flag set already on disk (evidence/profiles.json) and the registry already downloaded (~/.cache/gems62_registry, rebuilt by scripts/build_registry.sh).

What would have to be true for this lane to win a slot

  1. A metre-scale pick of the berms (H62-E), because at 100 m the shoreline is sub-pixel and the per-dot credit collapses.
  2. Or a per-flag screen that raises precision enough to clear the marginal bar α·s ≈ 0.0556 per dot at a useful dot count — H62-B is the test.
  3. Or a rebound model good enough that the residual is tectonic rather than fitted — H62-C.
