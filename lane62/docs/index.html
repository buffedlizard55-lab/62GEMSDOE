Why did the dotted H19-5 family score ~0.26, and why did thinning to 37,654 dots reach 0.2778?

The metric is a budget, and the identity DTI = T / (0.2·T + 0.2·F + 0.8·K) (K = withheld truth pixels) is exact. Every unit of prediction mass that is not the best cover of a truth pixel costs 0.2 and can earn at most 1. Thinning a thick surface while keeping its geometry removes mass that was already covered, raising credit per emitted pixel. Two independent routes to the break-even credit per dot: the metric algebra gives α·s = 0.2 × 0.2778 = 0.0556; the group measured ≈0.0548 empirically. This repository re-derives the bar from the published formulas and uses it as the selector on every candidate.

Can this lane beat 0.2778?

No, and the measurement that says so is the point of this session. The lane's most selective dot class — one dot per flagged datum break — carries mean credit 0.161 per dot, well above the bar, but it is only ≈90 dots, which cannot move a denominator of 0.8·K ≈ 6,300. At the mass end the credit per dot collapses (0.0401 at 35,651 dots). Critically, the same-instrument control (Results) shows a registered prior field reaching 0.293 with 45,000 dots — roughly double this lane's best dot class and 500× the count. The lane is dominated; it does not get a slot.

The lane, in one paragraph

The Sehoo (Lahontan) highstand shoreline formed at 13,070 ± 60 14C yr B.P. = 15,475 ± 720 cal yr B.P. on a near-level water plane (Adams & Wesnousky 1998). Because it was level when formed, its present elevation about a smooth isobase is a strain measurement: a fault that breaks the datum produces a step or a lateral deflection in it. That is the mechanism this lane exploits — and it is also why the lane is small: the faults that offset the Lahontan shoreline are, for the most part, the ones already mapped (Pyramid Lake, Wassuk, Smith Valley), so the lane buys defensibility rather than area.
